# 0019: Baton at the login window: a small Baton starts with the Mac, shows the login window to a paired phone and passes its input

Status: proposed. Written before any code, on the person's go. Nothing in it is built, and the two points that decide whether the picture and the typing work, L1 and L2 below, have not been tried on a Mac yet.

## Context

[ADR 0017](0017-away-from-home.md) lets a phone give the password of an account to a Mac that restarted with FileVault on, over SSH, so that the Mac can start. On 2026-11-11 the person tried it at home on one Mac (macOS 27.2, FileVault on, Remote Login on, a build signed with the Baton Signing certificate). The Mac did not sign the account in. Its logs show that the system was up and showing its login window at about 07:52:58 IST, and that nobody signed in until the person did it by hand at 07:56. In those minutes the phone could reach the Mac's SSH door (**Check the way** said that the way is open), so the network was up at the login window, and the phone had nothing of Baton to talk to. Baton for Mac is a login item of one account (`SMAppService.mainApp`, [architecture.md](../architecture.md)), so it starts when that account has signed in and not before. The part of the start where a password is taken is not in the Mac's logs, so that the unlock itself came from the phone is the likeliest reading and not a measured one.

The person asked for the missing part: a service of Baton that starts with the Mac, before anyone signs in, so that the phone can come in, with a setting that turns it off.

What is known, and how well:

- Nothing documented signs an account in while FileVault is on. The ways that were found are FileVault off with automatic sign-in, a screen sharing client, and a plug-in of the authorisation (reported: Apple's documentation and the answers of Apple's engineers on the developer forums, as the research of this project read them on 2026-11-11). Automatic sign-in is switched off by FileVault, an authenticated restart (`fdesetup authrestart`) skips the disk password once and then stops at the login window, and no command of device management that signs a person in was found (not tried here).
- FileVault itself signs the account in after a password that was typed at the Mac's own start screen, unless `DisableFDEAutoLogin` is set (reported). It was not set on the person's Mac, and the unlock over SSH did not sign anyone in (observed once).
- An engineer of Apple says on the developer forums (thread 814152) that a screen sharing product loads one launch agent for both the `Aqua` and the `LoginWindow` session types, that the agent captures the screen and posts the events, that capture before sign-in works on macOS 14.4 and later, and that a daemon must not touch the window server (reported, by an engineer; this project did not read the thread again).
- Two developers report that events posted from an agent in the login window did not arrive (threads 62687 and 724740, neither answered by Apple). Apple changed the Screen Recording policy in macOS 15 and did not publish the new rule (thread 768146), and the entitlement that screen sharing products may ask for needs the paid developer program. Whether capture and typing at the login window work for a build that is signed with the Baton Signing certificate, on macOS 27, is not known.
- The Mac app is sandboxed ([the entitlements](../../apps/baton/macos/Runner/Release.entitlements)), so it cannot write a system folder or start a program as root. A second program is built here the way the guard of the privacy mode is: by a script of the app's build that puts it into the app next to the app's own program (`apps/baton/macos/Guard/build-guard.sh`).
- A pairing is put to a person, who compares a code on both devices, and nothing outside the tests approves for them ([ADR 0006](0006-pairing-admission-and-requests.md)).

## Decision

**A small Baton of its own starts when the Mac shows its login window.** It is one program, `baton-loginwindow`, which launchd starts as a launch agent for the `LoginWindow` session type (a file in `/Library/LaunchAgents`, run at load and kept alive). It runs from the moment the disk is unlocked and the login window is up, as root, and the system ends it when someone signs in (all inferred; L3 and L5 look). It is the Rust library of the app without a screen of its own (`baton-app`, with the platform of the Mac for capture and input, as in [ADR 0009](0009-window-streaming.md) and [ADR 0010](0010-sessions-that-the-viewer-starts.md)), and it keeps what it needs in a folder of its own that only root can read. A launch daemon is not used: a daemon must not touch the window server, a daemon and an agent with a way between them would be more code with the same reach, and nothing here has to outlive the login window. Splitting the program into a part that listens, without privileges, and a part that captures and posts, is a later hardening, if the checks show that the agent can be split.

**It is a device of its own, and a phone pairs with it like with any device.** It has a key of its own and a list of devices of its own, and the phone shows it as another device, `<name of the Mac> (login window)`. The pairing is done in Terminal at the Mac: the program prints a ticket and puts it on the clipboard, the person gives it to the phone (**Add a device**, **Enter code**), and the person compares the six digits in Terminal, where the person answers, since nobody else is at a Mac that has no screen of its own for it. The key and the list of the app are not shared, for three reasons. The app's folder is in its sandbox, where a program that is not the app is kept out. A copy of the key would make two programs one device, which answer in turn. And a copy of the list would go on trusting a device that the person unpaired in the app. With a list of its own, forgetting the device at the phone, `baton-loginwindow forget` and the remove script each end the trust.

**It offers the main screen with control, and nothing else.** To a device that it paired with it gives the main display, and passes the events of the phone to the login window, as a session that the phone starts ([ADR 0010](0010-sessions-that-the-viewer-starts.md)); the pairing sets the `start` setting of that device to control, and nothing in the program changes it. It takes no links, text or files, reads nothing of what the person works on, and does not unlock anything. It asks nobody, since nobody is signed in to be asked. It does not cover the display or hold the keyboard of the Mac as the privacy mode does ([ADR 0011](0011-privacy-mode.md)): nothing private is on a login window, and a hold there could lock the person out. The phone's own rule stays: it asks for the person's face before it starts a screen session ([security.md](../security.md)), whatever the Mac is.

**It is installed by the person, with an administrator's password, as plain files, and removed the same way.** The app stays sandboxed and does not do it. A card in the Settings of the app says what the program does and shows one line to paste in Terminal. The line runs, with `sudo`, a script that is in the app (`Contents/Resources/login-window/install.sh`): it copies the program into `/Library/Application Support/Baton/LoginWindow/`, owned by root and writable by no one else (a program that root runs must not sit where the processes of a person can replace it), checks its signature, writes the launch agent file, and starts the pairing. `uninstall.sh` removes the agent, the folder and with it every pairing, and is the switch that turns it off. The newer in-app way (`SMAppService.daemon`) is not used: it is unproven with the Baton Signing certificate and asks for an approval in System Settings, while plain files need neither that nor an Apple account.

**No password is stored. The person types it on the phone.** The phone shows the login window and passes taps and keys, and the person types the password of the account as at the Mac. Typing it for the person, with the box of [ADR 0013](0013-unlock-from-a-phone.md) opened by the face, is a later change that needs L1 to pass and the box in the folder of the program, set up in Terminal; it has its own pull request and is read against that record.

**After the sign-in the session ends, and the phone goes on with the normal Baton.** The login window goes away when someone signs in, and the system takes the program with it. The phone's session ends with it, and the person opens the Mac under its own name. A later change makes the phone do that itself after the unlock: wait for the login window device, open it, and open the normal Mac when it ends. A gap remains. From the sign-in until the Baton of the account has started and covered the display, the desktop shows on the display of the Mac, which is against the rule of [ADR 0011](0011-privacy-mode.md) for a Mac that another device controls. The same program loaded for the `Aqua` session too can cover the display from the sign-in until the app takes over; that is a later change, and until it is built the guide names the gap.

**What the program may do, and what it may not.** It runs as root and listens on the network before anyone signs in. It listens as every Baton does: a stranger is closed after the handshake, pairing is refused unless the person opened an offer in Terminal a moment ago, and only a device on its own list can ask for a session ([ADR 0006](0006-pairing-admission-and-requests.md)). It opens no shell, runs no command, reads and writes nothing outside its own folder and the screen and input of the login window, logs no key that is typed (the log says that a session began and ended, and nothing more), and does not run in any session but the login window. launchd starts it again if it dies.

**If the two unproven points fail.** L1 (events at the login window) and L2 (capture at the login window) decide. If capture is refused, the program cannot show the login window, and what is left is typing blind with the box of ADR 0013 into the name and password fields, which the login window can be set to show so that typing is predictable; that needs L1. If posted events do not arrive, neither control nor typing works, and the plug-in of the authorisation is what is left, which ADR 0013 turned down for what a faulty one does (no account can sign in until the Mac is repaired from recovery) and which gets a record of its own before anyone builds it. If both fail, the stopgap that works today stays: the screen sharing of macOS reaches the login window after the unlock.

## How it will be measured

Without Apple hardware, on every pull request: the Rust part runs against a platform that plays the Mac, with a pairing and each way it is refused, a device that was not paired, the `start` setting, no links, text or files, the permissions of the folder, and the stop on a signal. On a Mac, by the person at it, with the notes of the install thread (a Log Out shows the login window again, so L1 and L2 need no restart):

- **L1. Events at the login window.** A launch agent for the `LoginWindow` session type, signed with the Baton Signing certificate and given Accessibility in System Settings, posts three harmless letters. Does the password field show three dots? Does it differ when the login window shows name and password fields (`SHOWFULLNAME`), as it does for a Mac with several accounts?
- **L2. Capture at the login window.** The same kind of agent captures one picture of the main display. Does it, does the system ask for a permission that cannot be given at the login window, and does it still work after a restart?
- **L3. The start after the unlock.** Restart the Mac and tap **Unlock** on the phone. Does the agent start at all after the unlock over SSH, how long after the first line of the system's log, and how long until the phone can reach it?
- **L4. The pairing in Terminal.** The ticket on the clipboard and on the phone, the codes, the refusal of a pairing that nobody offered, and what the program does when the phone gives up half way.
- **L5. The sign-in from the phone.** Tap the account, type the password, press Return: how long from the tap to the desktop, and what the phone shows when the login window goes away.
- **L6. The gap.** After the sign-in, how long does the desktop show on the display of the Mac before the app covers it, when the phone starts its session at once?
- **L7. The end.** The remove script leaves no file, no agent and no pairing, and the phone says that the device does not answer. A person at the Mac signs in as usual with the agent installed and with the agent killed.
- **L8. Away.** L3 and L5 through the route of ADR 0017.

## Alternatives considered

- **The key and the list of the app.** The phone would see one Mac, and nothing would have to be paired again. The folder of the app is not readable by another program (macOS keeps the sandbox container of an app from programs that are not the app: inferred, not tried here), a copy of the key makes two programs one device, and a copy of the list outlives an unpairing. It was the first shape of this record.
- **A launch daemon and an agent, with a way between them.** It is the shape that Apple's engineer describes. It would let the connection outlive the login window, which nothing here needs yet, and it would put a second program and an interface between two programs into the code that listens as root. The agent alone is the smaller thing to read, and the split stays open as a hardening.
- **`SMAppService.daemon`.** Unproven with the Baton Signing certificate, and it needs an approval in System Settings. Plain files in `/Library` need none.
- **A plug-in of the authorisation.** The most direct way, since it hands macOS the password with no typing and no screen permission, and the most dangerous: a faulty plug-in can leave no account able to sign in. Plan B, with a record of its own, if L1 fails.
- **The screen sharing of macOS, reached from Baton.** The phone would need a client of the protocol that macOS speaks, the Mac would have one more door open, and the session would not have the face of the phone's own rule or the input rules of Baton. It stays the stopgap that the guide names.
- **Automatic sign-in, with FileVault off or the password in a scrambled file.** The first gives up the disk's protection, the second keeps the password where a thief of the Mac could read it, and the person's rule is that no password lies there.
- **An authenticated restart.** It covers only restarts that the Mac itself starts, and stops at the login window.
- **Device management.** No command of it signs a person in.
- **The Mac App Store.** A sandboxed app cannot install a launch agent or write `/Library`. Baton for Mac stays a direct download.

## Consequences

- **A program that runs as root listens on the network before anyone signs in.** It answers like every Baton, and it can show and steer a login window for a device that the person paired with it in Terminal. Someone who holds a paired phone and gets the owner's face on it reaches the login window, and what stands in the way is the password of an account and the delay that macOS puts after wrong ones.
- **Installing needs an administrator's password and Terminal.** The app cannot do it, and says so on its card.
- **The phone lists the Mac twice**, once under its name and once with `(login window)`.
- **Nothing at the Mac says that a phone is looking at the login window.** The pointer moves, and that is all a person there sees.
- **The login window of a Mac that has no display attached may have nothing to capture** (reported for another product, not tried here).
- **A Wi-Fi network whose password is kept in the login keychain of the person cannot be joined at the login window** (inferred).
- **Every macOS release can change what the login window allows.** L1 and L2 are done again after an upgrade.
- **Sharing Baton for Mac with other people needs Developer ID signing and notarisation**, which is the paid program. The person's own Mac does not.
- **ADR 0017's open point after the unlock is answered** by the observation above, and by this record for what to do about it.
