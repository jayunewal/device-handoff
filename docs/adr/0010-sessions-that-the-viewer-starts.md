# 0010: Sessions that the viewer starts: allowed once at the Mac, asked for from the phone

Status: proposed. Built in the app on `main`; the checks on real devices in this record are not all run yet.

## Context

[ADR 0009](0009-window-streaming.md) starts every session at the source. The person goes to the Mac, picks a window or a display in the system's picker, picks a paired device and offers, and the phone says yes or no. A viewer that makes a Mac start capturing was left out on purpose, as the pull case, to be added later as a policy that is off by default.

The first tries on real devices showed why it is wanted. The person who owns the Mac and the phone is away from the Mac when they want to use it, and a share that can only begin at the Mac is of no use to them then. What they asked for is that the phone can turn on the Mac's sharing by itself.

What makes this a decision and not only a feature is the grant. Offering a window to a phone that the person holds is an act of the person at the Mac, made while they look at it. Letting a phone make the Mac capture its screen is a standing permission: the Mac acts on a request from another device and no one at the Mac is asked. This record has to say what stands in the place of the person's yes for each offer, and what that costs.

## Decision

**The viewer asks, the source has decided beforehand, and the offer is the offer of ADR 0009.** Three messages join the `Stream` requests (protocol.md section 9). The viewer sends `Start`: a session id that it chose, what it would like to see (`Screen`, the main display of the source, which the source chooses) and whether it would like to control it. The source answers `Starting` and then makes an ordinary `Offer` under that id, or answers `NotStarted` with a reason that a person can be told. Everything after the offer is unchanged: the answer, the frames, the control streams and the `Stop`. What is shared, and with what control, is still said by one message, and the code that streams does not know how a session began. A source that does this advertises `stream.start`, and a viewer sends a `Start` only to a device that did.

**The yes is given once, at the Mac, for one paired device, and it is off.** The settings for each paired device gain a setting, `start`, with three values: `Off`, `Look` and `Control`. It is `Off` for a device when it is first paired (pairing it again keeps the setting as it is), and in a file that was written before the setting existed. Only the person at the Mac sets it, in the settings for that device. No message changes it, and a paired device that asks while it is `Off` is told `NotAllowed` and nothing else. `Look` lets the device start a session to look at the screen, and `Control` lets it also control what it sees. The Mac reads the setting when a `Start` arrives and not when the connection began, so a change at the Mac holds from the next start.

**Control is the least of three.** A session has control only if the viewer wished for it in the `Start`, the setting for the device is `Control`, and the offer that the Mac makes says `control`. The viewer then takes no more than it wished for. A viewer cannot get from a Mac more than its person allowed, and a Mac cannot give a viewer control that its person did not allow.

**The viewer takes without asking only the offer that answers its own start.** The person at the viewer has just asked, by pressing the button, and asking them again would make the feature of no use. So a viewer that sent a `Start` takes an offer that comes from that device, under that id, of the kind it asked for (`Display`, for `Screen`), while the start waits for it (30 seconds in the reference implementation). Every other offer goes to the person as before, also one from the same device that comes a moment late or under another id. A Mac therefore cannot use the setting to put a session on a phone that did not ask for one.

**The limits of ADR 0009 stay.** There is one session at a time on each side. A `Start` is a request from a paired device and counts as one item against its limit, so a device cannot start sessions faster than it can send anything else, and a start that the settings refuse costs nothing, since the source does nothing for it. An id is used once on a connection, and the same start on another connection is a retry, because the answer to the first can be lost with its connection. The application of the source has 20 seconds to get ready (the reference implementation). A viewer that is told `Starting` and does not get the offer takes its asking back with a `Stop`, and the source gives up the offer.

**The Mac still shows what is shared.** A session that a phone started shows on the Mac as any other does: which device is viewing, what is shared, whether it controls, and a Stop button, and it says that the device started it itself. The Mac app does this when it has the means to capture a screen without the system's picker, in the pull requests that follow this record.

## Alternatives considered

- **Ask the person at the Mac for each start.** It is the rule of ADR 0009, and it is of no use to a person who is away from the Mac, which is the case this exists for.
- **Let the phone prove itself, with a check on the phone, and set nothing at the Mac.** It moves the yes to a device that can be stolen, and it needs a way for the Mac to know that the check was made. The person chose to give the yes at the Mac. The setting can take another value later, for a device that has proved itself, without changing the messages.
- **A setting that runs out.** It narrows the time in which a stolen phone could use it. It would also need the person to be at the Mac to renew it, which is the problem. It is a candidate for a later change.
- **Let the viewer choose the display.** `Screen` is the main display, and the source chooses. Choosing among displays needs a way for the viewer to learn what there is. A `wanted` that this version does not know is read as `Other` and answered as unsupported, so a later version can add it.

## Consequences

- A paired device that the person set to `Look` can see everything on the screen of the Mac whenever the Mac is awake and unlocked and Baton is running, and one set to `Control` can act as the person on all of it, system shortcuts included (ADR 0009). No one at the Mac is asked. That is the purpose of the setting and its cost. What limits it is that it is off, that it is for one device, that looking and controlling are separate, that only the person at the Mac can change it, that the Mac shows who is viewing and can stop it, and that the system shows an indicator while a screen is captured (documented for a screen that was shared through the picker; whether it shows for a capture without the picker is checked with the Mac side).
- A phone that was stolen or left unlocked, and that was set to start sessions, can start one. The pairing is the same trust as for handoffs, and the lock of the phone is what protects it. Whether the phone asks for authentication before it starts a view, as [locked-devices.md](../locked-devices.md#52-stage-before-leaving-and-do-not-read-a-locked-device) asks of a phone action that starts a send, is for the app on the phone and is decided with its button, not here. Unpairing the device at the Mac removes its setting with it and closes its connections (protocol.md section 3).
- A capture with no picker needs the consent of the system to record the screen, and the capture of what the person picked does not. The consent is the system's, given to the app in System Settings, and the Mac cannot give it for itself. Until it is given the Mac captures nothing and answers `NeedsPermission`. The app asks the system for it when the person at the Mac saves a setting that lets a device look or control, because a question that first comes up when a phone asks is shown to a Mac that no one may be at; it asks once more, once in each run of the app, when a phone asks for a screen and the consent is still missing. The system may take the consent into effect only when the app is opened again, and a build that is signed ad hoc may lose it with each build, as it loses the consent to post input events. The consent stands in the place of the picker for a screen that nobody picks, and it is a second condition beside the setting for the device: a phone that was allowed gets nothing from a Mac that was not. The system may also ask again. Since macOS 15 it has been reported to ask about once a month, about an app that captures the screen without the system's picker, with a question that says that the app wants to bypass the picker; this has not been checked on later versions. If it does ask, a start from a phone needs someone at the Mac about once a month, and while the question is up the listing of the displays may take longer than the five seconds that the Mac waits for it, so that the phone is told `Failed` and not `NeedsPermission`. Apple is said to give apps that share the screen an entitlement, on request, that is meant to spare the person that question; Baton does not ask for it, and this has not been checked either. The research on locked devices also has a report from a beta of macOS 27: the system shows a consent alert at every call that lists the displays, and there is a separate entry for remote desktop in Privacy & Security, which one vendor says spares the monthly question when it is granted (docs/research/locked-devices/apple.md, under "Consent and re-prompting"). It is the report of one beta. On the first try on a Mac with macOS 27.0.1 no alert came up at the starts that followed the one where the permission was given and Baton was opened again, so the report did not hold there; that is one Mac over a short time. Where it holds, each start from a phone needs someone at the Mac.
- The setting has no end date, and turning it off does not end a session that runs. It does not stop an offer that the Mac is already making either: a start that was answered `Starting` before the person turned the setting off still begins. A person ends a session with its Stop, and unpairing ends all the sessions of that device.
- Nothing here wakes or unlocks the Mac. A Mac that is locked or whose display is off cannot be captured (ADR 0009, locked-devices.md section 5.8), so a start then gives no picture, and the rule that Baton unlocks nothing on its own ([section 5.5](../locked-devices.md#55-unlocking)) is not changed by this record. [ADR 0013](0013-unlock-from-a-phone.md) adds the one case.
- Older devices are not affected. `Start`, `Starting` and `NotStarted` carry data, so a device from before this record cannot read them, but a viewer sends a `Start` only to a device that advertised `stream.start`, and an older device never does. The new `Wanted` and `WhyNot` are unit variants, and an older reader takes a value it does not know as `Other`.
- ADR 0009 said that a viewer cannot start a session on a Mac or ask for control. It points here now: a viewer can do so only for a device that the person at the Mac allowed beforehand, and what it asks for is a wish and not a grant.
- The work is split so that each pull request can be read on its own: the messages, the setting and the core (the first), then the Mac's means to capture a screen without the picker and the answer of its application to a `Start`, then the switch at the Mac and the button on the phone.
