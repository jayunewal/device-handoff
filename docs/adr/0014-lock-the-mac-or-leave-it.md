# 0014: Lock the Mac or leave it: the person who ends a private session at the other device is asked

Status: proposed. Built in the app on `main`; none of the checks on real devices in this record has been run yet.

## Context

[ADR 0011](0011-privacy-mode.md) keeps a Mac that another device controls dark and held, locks it when the session ends whatever the cause, and says that a Mac that stays unlocked is a security failure. [ADR 0013](0013-unlock-from-a-phone.md) lets the person unlock the Mac from the other device and repeats the rule: a session that was unlocked still ends locked. The rule has no exception, and the person met what that costs when they tried the first builds on their own devices (2026-10-26). They controlled the Mac from the iPad and ended the session, and the Mac locked. That is right when they walk away. It is not when they end the session because they are about to sit at the Mac, where they have to type the password they had just spared themselves.

They asked for a question at the other device when they end the session, whether to log out or not, and set two conditions. The answer is theirs at the other device, and nobody has to do anything at the Mac ("I should be able to do this from remote device and should not need to perform action on host"). And nobody else may be able to use a Mac that was unlocked from the iPad ("otherwise it can become a security disaster"). This record takes "log out" as "lock": a log out of the account would end the programs of the person's session, Baton among them (inferred), so the Mac could not be reached from the other device again until somebody logged in at it. It was offered as another choice and not taken.

What is known:

- A viewer ends a session today by finishing its control stream, and a source reads the end of that stream as the end of the session and locks (protocol section 9, Control streams). A stream delivers its messages in the order in which they were written, and its end after the last, so a viewer can say one more thing on it before it finishes it, and the source reads that first.
- What a viewer says to the source while a session runs travels on the streams that go one way, because the connection of a session is the one that the source opened to the viewer to offer its screen, and a node answers the requests that arrive on the connections it accepted and on no other. A request that the viewer opens on the connection of the session is never read by the source. This was found with two real nodes, after a first design that used a request (Alternatives considered).
- Everything that ends a private session at the Mac, or without the person at the viewer, is one that the person cannot be asked about: the three presses of Escape, a key at the Mac, the guard, a part that cannot be put back, a quit of the app, a lost capture, a connection that closes, a viewer that has gone quiet, an unpaired device.

## Decision

**The person who ends a private session at the viewer is asked whether to lock the source, and the answer is theirs alone.** When the person presses the end of a private session on the iPad or the iPhone, and the offer said that the source can leave its screen as it is (`leave`, below), the app asks "Lock the Mac?" with three answers. **Lock** is the default, and ends the session as every session has ended, with the Mac locked. **Leave it** ends the session and asks the Mac not to lock. **Cancel** does not end the session. The question is asked every time, and the answer is kept nowhere. It is not kept at the Mac, where the person said they should not have to do anything, and it is not kept at the other device either, because a stored "leave it" would be one consent given once for a Mac that is left open at every end after that. The question is not asked where it has no meaning: when the offer did not say `leave`, when the session is not private, and when the Mac is shown as locked (paused with `Locked`, which the viewer shows as a card over the picture), where there is nothing to lock and the Mac stays locked whatever the answer. If the word of the Mac is out of date and it was unlocked, the end is the ordinary one, which locks. The text says that anyone at the Mac can use it until it is locked, and, when the unlock was done from this device, that it was.

**Everything else locks.** Only the person at the viewer can leave the Mac unlocked, once, with the end that they pressed and the answer that they gave. Any other end is ADR 0011's: an app of the viewer that is closed, killed or hung, an end of the app that is not the person's answer (it goes to the background, the screen is turned off), a connection that closes, a viewer that has been quiet for 30 seconds, an unpaired device, and every end that begins at the Mac. A viewer of an older version ends the session the way it always did, and the Mac locks. A source of an older version never says `leave`, so it is never asked. A `Leave` that does not arrive, or comes after the session ended some other way, changes nothing: the first end of a session is the only one.

**Leaving puts everything back and locks nothing.** At the Mac, an end that leaves does what an end does before the lock (ADR 0011): it puts back what the person at the viewer lifted, lowers the cover, releases the hold of the keys and the pointer, lets go of the capture of the sound, and tells the guard that it is done. It does not lock. The screens light and the Mac is as it was in the picture the person last saw. A Mac that is locked when the session ends stays locked, and nothing is unlocked. A part that cannot be put back ends in the lock, as it does now: the Mac does not stay open because something in the end went wrong.

**The offer says `leave`, and the viewer asks with `Leave`.** The offer of a private session has one more key, `leave`, that a source sets when its platform can leave its screen as it is. It is left out otherwise, and a source does not set it without `private`, because a session that is not private does not lock the source at its end. The viewer takes it as it is, and a viewer of an older version ignores it. To leave, the viewer writes a new message, `Leave`, as the last message on its control stream, and then finishes the stream as it does at the end of every session. The source reads the messages of a stream in the order in which they were written and the end after the last, so it reads `Leave` before it reads the end. Nothing is waited for, and nothing answers.

**The source leaves the screen only where all of this holds**: the offer of the session said `leave`, the viewer wrote `Leave`, it wrote nothing after it, and it then finished its control stream. The source then ends the session as it ends one whose viewer finished its stream, except that it does not lock. A `Leave` in a session whose offer did not say `leave` is a rule broken by the viewer, as is a message after it, and the session ends with the lock. A stream that is reset after `Leave`, a connection that closes, and every other end lock as ever: the person chose, but the end that followed is not the one they chose, and a viewer that crashed on its way out says nothing of whether the person still wants the Mac open. A source of an older version never says `leave`, so it is never sent a `Leave`.

**This changes two lines of earlier records, which are not rewritten.** ADR 0011 says that a Mac that stays unlocked is a security failure, and means a Mac that stays unlocked because nobody chose it. ADR 0013 says that a session that was unlocked still ends locked, and means that it does unless the person at the viewer answered "Leave it". The person who unlocked the Mac from the iPad and then leaves it is the person the rules were for.

**What goes on the wire, and where the code lives.** One key in the offer, `leave`, and one message on the control stream of the viewer, `Leave`. No new capability: the offer says it, for the session, as it says `private`. Section 9 of the protocol specifies them. The offer is in `baton-proto` and the message in `baton-stream`. The rules (who may leave, which end is the one that was asked for) are in `baton-core`, tested with two real nodes on loopback and a platform that plays the Mac. The interface to the platform grows by one function, as the lift did: a platform that can leave gives it, a platform that does not is accepted as one that cannot, and the session then has no `leave`. Swift on the Mac ends the privacy without the lock. The dialog is Dart.

## How it will be measured

Without Apple hardware, on every pull request: the key and the message have known encodings and are fuzzed; the rules run against a platform that plays the Mac, including every end that must not leave (a viewer that resets its stream after `Leave`, a connection that closes, a `Leave` that the offer did not allow, a message after `Leave`, an end that begins at the Mac, a handle that is dropped, an end that comes first and a `Leave` that follows it), and on two real nodes, where the end that the person chose and the ends that they did not choose are told apart by what the platform of the Mac hears. A piece that only a Mac or an iPad can check is never described as tested.

On a Mac and an iPad, as in ADR 0009: record the builds, the models and the network. Each check is done by the person at the devices, with the steps in the pull request that adds the code.

- **E1. Leave.** In a private session on a Mac that is unlocked, by hand and then from the iPad with the face: press the end, answer "Leave it". The screens light at once, the keys and the pointer of the Mac work, the Mac does not lock, the sound of the Mac is back, the guard is gone, and Baton at the Mac is as it was before the session. How long from the answer to a Mac that can be used. Do it with the screens and the keys lifted, and with neither. When the Mac was unlocked from the iPad with the face, the question says that it was.
- **E2. Lock.** The same, answering "Lock": the Mac locks, the cover stays until it is locked, as in ADR 0011.
- **E3. Cancel.** The dialog does not stop the picture, the input, the sound or the reports, and a session that is left with the dialog open for a minute is still the same session.
- **E4. An end while the dialog is open.** Drop the connection, kill the app of the iPad, and press Escape three times at the Mac, each while the dialog is open. The Mac locks (or the session ends as it does at the Mac), and the dialog goes with a sentence that says the session is over.
- **E5. A locked Mac.** Lock the Mac, with the session open on it, and press the end while the iPad shows the card that says that the Mac is locked: no question, the session ends, the Mac stays locked, and nothing is woken or unlocked.
- **E6. An older viewer.** A build of the iPad without the dialog ends the session of a build of the Mac with it: the Mac locks.
- **E7. No connection.** Block the network the moment "Leave it" is pressed. The Mac locks, or it leaves if `Leave` and the end of the stream reached it before the connection broke, and the iPad says that the Mac was asked and does not say that it left. Which of the two happens at each moment, and how often the Mac left when the connection was already gone from the iPad.
- **E8. The put-back.** With a part that cannot be put back (the test build makes the cover refuse to move), answering "Leave it": the Mac locks.

## Alternatives considered

- **A setting at the Mac, "let this device leave the Mac unlocked".** The person said that nothing is to be done at the Mac. A setting would also be a promise of the Mac to leave itself open, kept on its disk, for every session after it.
- **A setting at the other device that remembers the answer ("always leave", "always lock").** "Always lock" is the answer that is asked for today. "Always leave" turns a question into a standing consent. The answer is asked each time, and the one that is easy to give is the safe one.
- **A `Stop` with a new key `after`, answered with `Stopped`.** The first design, since a source already takes a `Stop` for a session that runs. It has an answer, so the person could be told that the Mac took it. It does not work. The connection of the session is the one that the Mac opened, and a node answers the requests that arrive on the connections it accepted and on no other, so a `Stop` that the iPad opens on that connection is never read. A `Stop` on the connection that the iPad opened to the Mac would arrive, but on another connection than the end of the control stream, with no order between the two. A viewer would have to finish its stream only after the answer, with a wait for it, and a source that read the end first would lock. A message on the stream that is already there has the order for free and needs no wait.
- **A message that is not the last.** The viewer could say `Leave` earlier, as a standing wish for the end, and finish its stream whenever it ends. Then a `Leave` that the person took back (a Cancel) would have to be taken back as well, and a viewer that crashed after it would leave the Mac open. Said at the end, as the last message, it is the answer to the question, and only the person's end that follows it counts.
- **Leave as the default and lock as the choice.** The person asked for the Mac to lock, and the failure that ADR 0011 was written against is the Mac that stays open without a decision.
- **A log out of the account.** See Context. A later version can add it as another message of the viewer, say a `LogOut`, written the same way.
- **Leave offered in a session that is not private.** Such a session does not lock the source at its end, so there is nothing to ask.
- **The Mac locks again after some minutes.** It protects a person who forgets. The Mac already has the person's own settings for that (the screen saver and the time after which it asks for the password, inferred), and a timer of Baton's would lock a Mac that the person sat down at.

## Consequences

- **A Mac can end a session unlocked, at the choice of the person at the other device.** Anyone at the Mac can use it until it is locked, by the person or by the settings of the Mac. The dialog says so.
- **An iPad or an iPhone that someone else holds, with a session open, can answer "Leave it".** The same device could already control the whole Mac, and the face that the unlock asks for guards the unlock and not a session that is open (ADR 0013). Leaving adds the Mac being open to the people near it after the session, which is what the question exists to say.
- **Nothing is less safe when the person does not choose.** A viewer that does not ask, a source that does not offer, a `Leave` that does not arrive and every end that is not the person's answer leave the rule of ADR 0011 as it was.
- **The answer is not kept.** A person who leaves the Mac every time is asked every time.
- **The iPad cannot know that the Mac left its screen as it is.** Nothing answers `Leave`, so the iPad says that the Mac was asked, and not that it did. If the connection broke first the Mac locks, which is the safe way to be wrong, and the person who needs to know looks at the Mac.
- **A session that was unlocked from the iPad and then left shows the Mac as it was in the picture**: the desktop of the person, lit, with whatever was open.
- **The dialog is Dart, and the rules are Rust.** The dialog cannot make the Mac leave anything the library does not allow; the library does not ask.
