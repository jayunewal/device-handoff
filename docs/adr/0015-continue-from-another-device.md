# 0015: Continue from another device: the phone asks the Mac what is open, and the Mac reads it then

Status: proposed. Everything that can be built and checked without Apple hardware is built: the messages, the setting for each device, the node and the app layer, tested with two nodes on loopback, and the reading on a Mac, the setting in the menu of a device and the sheet on the phone, tested with a reader and a session that the tests play. The Swift code is compiled only by CI on a Mac, and the checks on real devices in this record are not run yet.

## Context

Baton hands things off from the device that is being used: the person sends a link, some text or files to another device. It cannot do the other thing. A person who sits down at the phone and wants what was in front of them on the Mac has to go back to the Mac, send it and come again. The review of Baton against its goal (2026-11-10) found this the thin half of handoff and put a way to pull first: the phone asks the Mac for the page that is open and the text that was copied, and opens or copies them with one tap.

What is known:

- Section 10 of the protocol and [locked-devices.md](../locked-devices.md) already plan a pull, as a list of what a device has ready (`ListActivities`) and a fetch of one of them (`Fetch`), over items that the device *stages*: it reads the clipboard and the tab as the person works, keeps them, and serves them later. That is what lets a locked or sleeping device answer, and it is also a device that reads what its person does all day and keeps it. Nothing in the app stages anything today.
- What a person is working on is private in ways that a title bar hides. A page address can carry a sign-in token, a title can name a document, and the clipboard can hold a password. The person has to have chosen who may read it, and a device that they did not choose must get nothing.
- Asking the person at the Mac is no use, because the phone asks when the person is not at the Mac. [ADR 0010](0010-sessions-that-the-viewer-starts.md) met the same thing for screens and settled it by allowing a paired device once, at the Mac.
- The Mac can read the page that is in front in Safari and in Chrome with Apple Events, which macOS lets a program do only after the person said yes to a question that names the browser, and the clipboard with `NSPasteboard`. Both are calls to a program that is running, so they can be made when somebody asks, and need no staging. How long they take is not known yet (C1).

## Decision

**The phone asks, and the Mac reads then.** A new request, `Continue`, carries an empty question, `Ask`, and the answer is `Items`: the page that is in front in the browser (an address and a title, as a link has) and the text on the clipboard, each left out when there is none. Nothing is read in advance, nothing is kept after the answer, and neither the node nor the app layer holds any of it: the platform of the Mac reads when the request arrives, and the answer is as old as the question. A device advertises `continue.serve` when its platform can read; a device that does not is told `Unsupported`.

**It is off until the person at the Mac turns it on, for each device.** The policy of a paired device has one more setting, `continuing`, off by default. Only the person at the Mac changes it, in the settings for that device, and nothing a paired device sends can. It is not written to the trust file while it is off, so a version that does not know it reads the file as it wrote it. A device that is not allowed is answered `Declined(Blocked)`, which the phone shows as "turn it on at the Mac", and it is not read for and does not count against the limit below. Unpairing removes the setting with the rest of the policy.

**Nothing is read that the person did not put in front of the Mac.** These are the rules for the platform, which the node cannot check and the interface says: a Mac that is locked, whose display is off, or whose screen another account has (Baton runs in each account, and the one that nobody is looking at answers as a locked Mac does) gives an empty `Items` and not what it last held, and says nothing of why, so that a phone cannot tell a locked Mac from one with nothing open; a page that a screen would not open in the web browser (`web_address`: a `file:` address, `about:blank`, the start page of a browser) is left out by the library before it goes on, and the text is still given, since there is nothing to continue in such a page and the name of a file says more than the person chose to share; clipboard text that its source marked as concealed or transient (password managers do) is never read; a private window of Chrome is left out; and the browser that is read is the one in front, or the one that was in front last, and one that is not running is not started. An answer with neither part is the right answer in each of these cases.

**An allowed device is limited more than a sender of items, and is read for one question at a time.** Once the device is allowed, a request counts against a limit of its own for each paired device, 6 a minute by default (`handoff.max_continues_per_minute`), and, when that lets it through, as one item against the limit for items (`handoff.max_items_per_minute`). The limit of its own is lower because each request reads the clipboard and nothing shows that it was read: with the limit for items alone, 30 a minute, a device that is allowed could ask every two seconds and take each text that the person copies. A request that the lower limit turns away costs no item, and the phone is told `RateLimited`, which it shows as busy. The node answers one request at a time, and a second while one is being read gets `RateLimited`. The platform has 5 seconds to read: one that takes longer is given up on and the phone is told `Other`, and the phone waits those 5 seconds and 10 more for the network. A page or a text beyond the limits of a link and of clipboard text is dropped by the node, and the other part is still given. The Dart code that answers holds to it too: one read of the platform is out at a time, which can be longer than the 5 seconds when a browser hangs or the question that the system puts to the person is waiting, and a question that comes meanwhile is told at once that it could not be read, so that nothing is queued behind the first read.

**The phone acts on a tap.** It shows what it was given as a card for the page and a card for the text, each with a button: Open, which goes through `web_address` as a received link does, so only `http` and `https`, and Copy. Nothing opens and nothing is copied before the tap, and nothing is kept in the inbox.

**How the Mac reads.** The Swift code asks Safari and Chrome for the address and the title of the page in front with Apple Events, from a queue of its own and one browser at a time, and gives each three seconds. It reads the clipboard with `NSPasteboard`. The browser that is read is the one in front or, when the person is in another app, the one that was in front last, if it is still open. A browser that is not running is never asked, because asking one by name starts it. When the browser cannot be read but the clipboard can, the phone is given the clipboard; the app says that it could not read only when it has nothing at all to give, which the phone shows as "could not be read just now" and not as "nothing to continue". A title that is too long for a link is cut by the Dart side, so that the page is not lost with it.

**The question that macOS asks is put at the Mac, while the person is there.** macOS asks the person whether Baton may control a browser only while that browser is open, and only a person at the Mac can answer. So when the person saves the setting for a device as on, Baton asks the system about each browser that is open, and says in the same breath where it stands: which browser it may read, which one the person refused and where to change that (System Settings, Privacy & Security, Automation), and which one is not open, so that macOS has not asked yet. The same happens each time they save the setting on, which is also how a consent that a new build lost is asked for again. A refusal leaves the clipboard working, and the settings text says so.

**What goes on the wire, and where the code lives.** One capability, one request and its reply, in section 3 of the protocol. `Ask` is a case of an enum and not a bare struct on purpose: a request is read by the name of its case, so a response can never be read as a request, which a struct of keys that a reader ignores would allow (the decoder ignores keys it does not know). A case that a version does not know is an error, as for every request. The messages and their limits are in `baton-proto`. The rules (the setting, the limit, one at a time, the time, what is given) are in `baton-core`, tested with two real nodes on loopback. `baton-app` gives the platform an event to read for (`ContinueAsked`) and a call to answer with (`answer_continue`), and the bridge copies them. The Swift code of the Mac reads (`MainFlutterWindow.swift`), a small server in the Dart code answers each `ContinueAsked` with what the Swift code read, and the Dart screens ask and show: a button on the row of a Mac, which opens the sheet, and an entry in the menu of the row of a device, which asks the person at the Mac.

## How it will be measured

Without Apple hardware, on every pull request: the messages have known encodings and are fuzzed, and the compatibility tests hold the rules that keep old and new versions apart (keys that are ignored, a reason that is not known, the two directions not read as each other, a part over its limit); the node is tested with two real nodes (a device that is not allowed is not read for and not counted, one question at a time, the time, the limits of what is given, a device with no way to read); the app layer is tested with a platform that the test plays, including a phone that gives up, a platform that is too slow, an app with nobody to read for it and a page that would not open. The Dart code is tested with a reader that the tests play: the server reads once for each question, starts no second read while one is out, gives a read that failed as "could not read", and answers nothing once it is closed; the sheet shows each way a question can end; and the menu asks the system only where the person saved the setting on. Tests read the Swift file, the entitlements and the property list for what the Dart code relies on: the channel, its questions and the words of its answers, the two browsers and no other app in the entitlements, the three seconds, the check for a Mac that nobody is using before anything is read, the marks of secret text, and that no browser is ever started.

On a Mac and an iPhone, as in ADR 0009, with the builds, models and network recorded. Each check is done by the person at the devices, with the steps in the pull request that adds the code.

- **C1. Safari.** Open a page, ask from the phone. macOS asks once whether Baton may control Safari; after the yes the phone is given the title and the address. How long from the tap to the cards, with the Mac's window closed.
- **C2. Chrome.** The same. A window in incognito is left out.
- **C3. A private window in Safari.** Whether Apple Events show it, and what the phone is given. The limit is written down in the settings text either way.
- **C4. A no.** Answer "Don't Allow" at the question. What the phone says, and that the settings for Automation in System Settings bring it back.
- **C5. Locked.** A locked Mac, a Mac with its display off, and the Baton of an account that another account has pushed off the screen give nothing, and the phone says that there is nothing to continue.
- **C6. Concealed.** Copy a password from a password manager: the phone is not given it. Copy plain text: it is.
- **C7. No window.** With the window of Baton closed and only the menu bar item left, ask from the phone.
- **C8. Off.** Turn the setting off at the Mac: the next ask is turned away without a question and without a read.
- **C9. The clipboard alert.** With text copied and nobody at the Mac, ask from the phone. Whether macOS puts up an alert for a read of the clipboard that no paste started (Apple describes one from macOS 15.4, with `NSPasteboard.accessBehavior` to say where the person's choice stands), and whether Baton's window stops answering until someone clicks it, because the read runs on the main thread. If the alert shows, the choice can be put to the person when they save, as it is for the browsers. If the read waits, it moves off the main thread.
- **C10. The three seconds.** Close the browser, turn the setting on for a phone, then open the browser and ask from the phone, which is the first read from it. Whether the script still gives up after three seconds while macOS asks whether Baton may control the browser, or whether that question holds the read until someone answers it. If it holds, the phone is told that it could not be read after five seconds and is not given the clipboard, and the sentence about the first read in the consequences is wrong.

## Alternatives considered

- **Stage on the Mac and serve later** (the plan in section 10). It answers while the Mac is locked or asleep, but it reads and keeps what the person does all day, needs a list, a fetch and a store with rules of their own, and serves what may be hours old. Asking at the time gets the same page with nothing kept. A Mac that is asleep gives nothing here, and the plan stays open for it.
- **Ask the person at the Mac each time.** Nobody is there.
- **Push what is open to the phone whenever it changes.** It keeps the phone's picture of the Mac fresh at the cost of battery and network, and shows what the person reads to a device that may not be in their hand. A pull is enough.
- **A browser extension that reports the tab.** It works in any browser, and needs one install for each browser and profile, a way to reach Baton and a listing in a store. Apple Events need one question. An extension remains the way to add Firefox and the others later.
- **A list of items in the reply.** It is more general, and each new kind would then need its own check of what the other device understands. Two named parts keep the limits plain, and a later version can add a part, which an older reader ignores.

## Consequences

- An allowed phone can read the address and title of the page in front in the Mac's browser and the text on its clipboard whenever the Mac is unlocked and Baton is running, at most 6 times a minute, for as long as the person leaves the setting on. The text of the setting says so. The Mac shows no notice when a phone reads in this version: the limit of 6 a minute was chosen for now instead (2026-11-11), since it needs no new screen, and a phone that keeps asking reads the clipboard every ten seconds at the most. The event that tells the platform to read names the device that asks, so a notice can be added without a protocol change.
- The Mac has to be running Baton. The menu bar item of the app, which stays on without a window (the change before this one), is what lets the phone's question reach a Mac that nobody sits at.
- Reading needs the Automation permission for each browser, and macOS ties it to the signature of the app. A build that is signed ad hoc and differently each time asks again; stable signing is a step of its own.
- The sandbox of the Mac app is given an exception to send Apple events to Safari and Chrome and to no other app (`com.apple.security.temporary-exception.apple-events` in both entitlement files, and `NSAppleEventsUsageDescription` in `Info.plist`, which is the text that macOS shows when it asks). A test holds the list at those two.
- A browser that was closed when the person turned the setting on has not been asked about yet, so macOS asks at the first read from it, which may be when nobody is at the Mac. That read is expected to give the clipboard alone, because the script gives up after three seconds (C10 checks that the question does not hold it longer), and the next one gives the page if the person said yes.
- Only a Mac reads. A device whose platform does not advertises nothing, and a phone that asks it is told that it cannot.
- The trust file gets one more optional key. It gives an unpaired device nothing.

## Open points

- A notice at the Mac when a phone reads: "Pocket Phone took the page that you were reading". The limit above bounds how often a phone reads, and a notice would show each read.
- Whether Safari shows its private windows to Apple Events (C3).
- Browsers other than Safari and Chrome.
- A row for Automation in the screen of permissions, next to Accessibility and Screen Recording, so that the person can see it and open its page in System Settings without turning the setting off and on.
