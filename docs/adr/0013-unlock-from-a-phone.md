# 0013: Unlock from a phone: the face on the phone opens a key, and the Mac types its own password

Status: proposed

## Context

[ADR 0009](0009-window-streaming.md) pauses the picture of a Mac that locks, since nothing documented lets a program capture a locked screen, and says that Baton never unlocks anything. [ADR 0011](0011-privacy-mode.md) keeps a Mac that another device controls dark and held, locks it when the session ends, and leaves unlocking from afar to the next record. This is that record.

The person uses their own Mac from an iPad or an iPhone while other people are at home. The Mac locks: its idle timer runs out, a hot corner is touched, or the person locks it. The session then says that the Mac is locked and goes no further, and the person has to walk to the Mac, which is what working from the other device was meant to spare them. They asked to unlock it from the other device, and set the conditions. The Mac must not light up or show anything while they do. Nobody else may use it once it is unlocked. Ending the session, or losing the connection, locks it again, and a Mac that stays unlocked is a security failure. On top of that, the password must not lie where a thief of the Mac or of the phone could read it, it must be the person's face on the phone that allows the unlock and not only the pairing, and the feature is off until the person turns it on at the Mac.

What is known, and how well:

- A program in the person's own session, with the consent for Accessibility, can post keys at the lock screen of macOS 27.0.1. On 2026-10-25 a test tool on the person's Mac typed three letters into the lock screen and the person saw three dots in the password field. A whole password and Return were not tried, and neither was a display that was off.
- Nothing documented lets a program unlock a Mac, and the unlock with a watch that the system offers is not open to apps (documented). A program that unlocks therefore types the password, as the apps that unlock a Mac from a phone are reported to do.
- The Secure Enclave of an iPhone and an iPad keeps a P-256 key that cannot leave it. A key can be made so that every use needs the person's face (`biometryCurrentSet`, Face ID, or Touch ID on a device that has no Face ID; "the face" stands for whichever the device has in this record), so that a new face ends it, and without the passcode as a way round. Security.framework seals a short value to the public half and opens it with the private half in the Secure Enclave (ECIES, documented). Baton has not run either yet; U5 and U6 below do.
- Baton keeps running while a Mac is locked, so it can take the request of the phone (inferred, backed by reports; the checks M1 and M10 of [locked-devices.md](../locked-devices.md) cover it). A Mac that is asleep, with its lid shut, or that has just restarted is out of reach, and so is every Mac before its first sign-in after a restart (documented).

## Decision

**The password is locked with the face, and the Mac keeps only what is useless without the phone.** When the person sets the feature up, the Mac makes a random key of 32 bytes, the box key, and encrypts the password with it (AES-256-GCM) into the box. It then seals the box key to a public key whose private half is in the Secure Enclave of the phone (ECIES on P-256). The Mac keeps the box, the sealed key and that public key, for that phone only. The public key is no secret, and the Mac needs it to seal the next box key (below). Nothing else is written down: the password and the box key are not kept anywhere on the Mac. The phone keeps no password, no box and no sealed key; it has the private key, which cannot leave the Secure Enclave and which works only after its person's face is recognised. The disk of the Mac does not open the password, the phone alone does not, and no passcode does. It takes both devices and the face, at every unlock, because a box key is used once (below), with the two exceptions that the Consequences name. The password is padded to 128 bytes before it is encrypted, so that the box does not say how long it is, and a password of more than 127 bytes is refused at the setup. The box is authenticated together with a context string and the ids of the two devices, so that a box taken from one record opens nothing in another.

**The person sets it up once at the Mac, for each phone, and it starts off.** In the menu of a paired device, next to looking and controlling (ADR 0010), the person chooses "Let this device unlock this Mac…", and a window asks for the password of the Mac in a field that hides what is typed. The window also says where the unlock stands (not set up, set up since when, or ended), turns it off, and lists the last attempts of that phone. Baton checks it against the account of the person before it keeps anything, so that a typing mistake is found at the setup and not as a failed login at the lock screen. Then the Mac asks the phone for its key with a `Key` request, and the phone answers with the public key it made for this Mac (it makes one if it has none). The Mac seals the box key to it, keeps the box, the sealed key and the public key in the record of the paired device, and wipes the password and the box key from memory. The phone replaces its key at every setup, so what was sealed to an older key opens nothing from then on, and a setup that stops after the phone made its new key turns the earlier one off and says so, instead of leaving a record that shows as on and fails at the first tap. The setting is changed at the Mac only and by no message. Turning it off, setting it up again and unpairing the phone each delete the box, the sealed key and the public key. A Mac that cannot check a password offers nothing, and so does a Mac whose lock does not take one.

**A session offers the unlock only when it is ready for it.** The offer says `unlock` when the person set the feature up for this phone, the session is private, and both devices advertised their capabilities (`unlock.target` at the Mac, `unlock.approve` at the phone). The phone shows the Unlock button in a session whose offer has `unlock`, and only while the Mac is locked. The offer says `unlock` whether or not the Mac is locked when it is made, so that a person who picks up the phone after the Mac locked itself can unlock it from there; whether a session can start on a locked Mac at all is not known (U11). A session without `unlock` has no unlock, also when the Mac could do it, and the offer cannot be changed later, as with control.

**Unlocking is a short exchange on the control streams, and the face opens the key in the middle of it.** The person taps Unlock and the phone sends `Unlock`, with a number for the attempt that the answers repeat, so that a late answer to an attempt that the phone gave up on is not taken for the answer to the next. The Mac checks that it can try (below) and sends `UnlockSealed`: the sealed key, and a challenge of 16 random bytes made for this attempt alone. The phone asks for the person's face, with a prompt that names the Mac, and the Secure Enclave opens the sealed key only if the face is recognised. The phone sends `UnlockOpened`: the challenge and the box key. Or it sends `UnlockCancelled` with a reason, and nothing is opened. The Mac drops an answer whose challenge it does not hold, so that a late answer or a repeated one does nothing. Otherwise it opens the box with the key, and the rest is the Mac's own, and it ends with `UnlockDone` and an outcome that says what came of it. An attempt that the phone cancels ends there, and the Mac says nothing more. The box key goes once, inside the connection, to the Mac that asked, and the password never leaves the Mac. The phone opens a sealed key only with the key it made for the Mac of the session and tries no other, since a sealed key does not name its key and a phone that tried its keys in turn would open one that was copied from another Mac. The challenge ties an answer to one question and does not protect the key. The key is a bearer key: whoever holds a copy of it and of the box can open the password. So a key is used once. The Mac replaces the box and the sealed key with new ones before it types (below), and a copy of the key that was used, made by a changed app on the phone or left in the buffers of the networking library, opens nothing that the Mac holds afterwards. That is why every unlock needs the face, and not only the first, with two exceptions that the Consequences name: a Mac that cannot keep its new box leaves the old one in place, and a phone can open a key and not send it. The key still travels in the encrypted connection only, and the Mac and the phone wipe what they own of it as soon as it has been used.

**The Mac types the password itself, once, and looks at the result.** With the box open it first puts the password into a new box under a new random key, seals that key to the public key of the phone that it keeps, and replaces the old box and sealed key with the new ones, in one step that either happens whole or not at all. If it cannot, it types nothing, the attempt ends with `Failed`, and the box it had stays, so that the person can try again. The key that was sent still opens that box until an unlock replaces it, which is one of the two cases in which a key is good for more than its own attempt (Consequences). Then it wakes the display if the display is off (a documented power call), checks that the session is locked, and types the password into the lock screen as keys and then Return. Each key goes into the record of what the app has posted, as the keys of the viewer do (ADR 0011), so that the tripwire, which ends a session at a key that the app did not post, stays quiet through the password. The lock screen takes keys that a program posts (see above). Keys that go into a password field are hidden from event taps (ADR 0011 measured it for a password field in an app, and the lock screen was not part of that check; U4 looks), so another program on the Mac is not expected to see the password go by. Before each key it asks the system whether the session is still locked and stops at the first key that is not for a locked screen, so that a password is never typed into a window; one key can still be in flight when the lock goes, which is the most a window can receive (inferred, U2 looks). It wipes both keys as soon as the new pair is kept and the password as soon as the last key is posted, and then it waits for the system to say that the session is unlocked, ten seconds at most. The typing bypasses the gate that drops input at a locked Mac (ADR 0009): that gate is for the keys of the viewer, and these are not those. The attempt ends at once if the session ends, if the viewer cancels, or if the lock goes.

**Only in a private session, with the cover and the hold in place.** This is the person's rule, and the Mac enforces it: the Mac tries only in a session whose offer set `private` and `unlock`, only while neither of the two switches of ADR 0011 has lifted the cover or the hold, and only while the platform says that the cover and the hold are in place and the guard is alive. A switch that is on makes the phone show the button as not usable and say why, and the Mac answers `NotPrivate` to an `Unlock` that comes all the same. A switch that is turned on while an attempt waits for the face ends the attempt with `NotPrivate`. A switch that is turned on while the password is typed takes effect when the typing is over, since the platform does both on one thread. The reason is the person's: the Mac must not light up or show activity while they work from the other device, and the unlock wakes a display and shows a lock screen. The cover is kept through the wake; a display that wakes can light for up to a quarter second before the cover is set again (ADR 0011), and U3 measures it.

**One try for each tap, and a try that fails ends the setup.** The Mac makes at most one attempt at a time and types a password at most once for each `UnlockSealed`. A password that the system does not accept (`Rejected`) ends the setup of that phone: the Mac marks the record as unusable, answers `NotSetUp` to every later `Unlock`, and tells the person at the phone to set it up again at the Mac, unless the screen proves to be unlocked within 20 seconds (a slow system, or the person unlocked it by hand), which gives the setup back. It never tries again by itself and does not try a second password. The same holds for a box that the key does not open (`Stale`), which happens before anything is typed, and for a phone that says it has lost its key. It holds too when the platform of the Mac says that it typed, that it unlocked, or that the screen was not locked or not private, without having put the password in a new box first: the key that was sent would still open the box, and a copy of it would be good for one more unlock without a face, so the Mac ends the setup, and does not give it back when the screen proves to be unlocked. If the disk of the Mac does not take the mark, the Mac remembers it until the program stops, and the setup is usable again after a restart. A wrong guess costs one failed login in the log of the Mac, and a stale password cannot cost more by being used again and again. Setting it up again is at the Mac and takes the password again.

**The person at the Mac is told, and a record is kept.** Each attempt, whatever came of it, is written to a short history at the Mac (the last hundred) with the time, the name of the phone as the person gave it, and the outcome, and nothing of the password, the keys or the challenge. The app posts a notification at the Mac for an unlock that succeeded and for one that failed, which the person sees when the screens are lit again. It names the phone, and for an unlock it says what to do if that was not the person: forget the phone in Baton and change the password of the Mac. The app asks the system for the right to show notifications when the unlock is first set up. Where the person said no, or the system cannot show one, the window of the unlock says so, and says where to allow them or, if the system cannot, that the list of attempts in it is where to look. A refusal that came before an attempt (`NotSetUp`, `NotPrivate`, `Busy`, `NotLocked`) is written to the history and posts no notification, since nothing was typed. Neither does an attempt that ended at the phone because its person did not agree, their face was not recognised or they cancelled (`Declined`), nor one that the end of the session cut short (`SessionEnded`): nothing was typed then either, and the history has them.

**A session that was unlocked still ends locked.** The unlock changes nothing in ADR 0011. The session stays private, the cover and the hold stay in place, and whatever ends the session, the viewer, the connection, a quit or a crash, locks the Mac again before the cover comes down. The phone cannot keep a Mac unlocked by dropping out of the session.

**What goes on the wire, and where the code lives.** Two capabilities, `unlock.target` and `unlock.approve`; the flag `unlock` in the offer; one request, `Key`, with which the Mac asks the phone for its public key; and five messages on the control streams, `Unlock`, `UnlockSealed`, `UnlockOpened`, `UnlockCancelled` and `UnlockDone`. Section 9 of the protocol specifies them. The messages are in `baton-proto` and `baton-stream`. The rules (who may ask, which conditions, the single attempt, the timeouts, the challenge) are in `baton-core`, tested with two real nodes on loopback and a platform that plays the Mac and the phone. The interface to the platform grows at each end, as the lift did: three functions on the Mac (check the password, set the unlock up, unlock a screen), three on the phone (make the key, ask for the face, take the question back), and five calls from the platform into the library (the privacy as it stands, whether an attempt goes on, keeping the new box, and the two answers of the person at the phone). A platform that does not know them is accepted as one that cannot unlock, and so is one that gives only some of the three at its end. Swift on the Mac keeps the box, checks the password, types it and wakes the display. Swift on the phone makes the key and asks for the face. Dart has the Unlock button, which is on a card over the picture of a Mac that is locked, the window of the setup and of the recent attempts, and all the sentences; the notification is Dart’s too, shown by Swift on the Mac over a channel. The work is split into pull requests that can be read one at a time: this record with the protocol and the messages, the rules, the app layer, the interface of the platforms, Swift on the Mac, Swift on the phone, and the bridge with the screens.

## How it will be measured

Without Apple hardware, on every pull request: the messages have known encodings and are fuzzed; the rules run against a platform that plays the two devices, including every refusal, a late answer, a repeated one, a challenge that is wrong, an attempt that outlives the session, a switch that is turned on in the middle, and the wrong cases of the capabilities; the box and the sealed key are made, opened and replaced in the Swift tests on a hosted Mac with a software key in place of the Secure Enclave, including a record that cannot be written; the screens (the Unlock button and its sentences, the window of the setup, the notification) run as widget tests against a fake library, and the calls of the app cross the bridge to the real library in the bridge tests. A piece that only a Mac or a phone can check is never described as tested.

On a Mac and a phone, as in ADR 0009: record the builds, the models and the network. Each check below is done by the person at the devices, with the steps in the pull request that adds the code.

- **U1. The password at the lock screen.** After the screen saver, with the display off, and with the display on: does the call that declares user activity wake the display from the sandbox, how long after the wake does the lock screen take its first key, and does the first key wake the screen and get lost. Does a whole password go in and does Return unlock it, for a password with capitals, digits, symbols, spaces, letters with accents and characters that need two UTF-16 units, on a keyboard layout other than the US one. How long from the tap on the phone to the unlocked Mac, and how long from the Return to the notice that the session is unlocked, since the Mac waits ten seconds for it and then calls the password rejected, and takes the unlock back if the screen proves to be unlocked within twenty seconds after that.
- **U2. The check of the lock between keys.** Unlock the Mac by hand, with Touch ID or with a watch while the password is typed. Where do the keys after that go, how many, and does any reach the program that has the keyboard.
- **U3. The cover through the unlock.** In a private session, from the lock to the unlocked Mac: is every display dark at every moment, how long does a display that wakes light, and are the lock screen and the desktop never seen. Does the notification of the unlock show only when the screens are lit again.
- **U4. The hold through the unlock.** While the password is typed: do a key and a click at the Mac get through, do the keys that the app posts pass the tap, and do three presses of Escape at the Mac still end the session. Does the tripwire stay quiet through the whole password, with the keys entered in the same record as the keys of the viewer.
- **U5. The key in the Secure Enclave.** Made with the face required and the passcode not allowed: the prompt shows the text that the app gives, a face that is not recognised three times opens nothing, the app in the background cannot use it, and after a face is added or removed, after the passcode is changed, and after the app is deleted and installed again, does the key still open, and what error does the system give each time.
- **U6. Sealing between the two devices.** The Mac seals to the 65-byte public key of the phone and the phone opens it, on an iPhone and on an iPad. The sizes of the sealed key. A vector made with a software key on both ends, so that the two sides agree on the format before the hardware is involved.
- **U7. Checking the password at the setup.** Whether the Mac can check a password against the account from the sandbox, with which entitlement or call, for an account that is local, one that is linked to an Apple account, and one that is managed. What it says for a Mac whose lock does not take a password, and for an empty password.
- **U8. A wrong password.** Change the password of the Mac after the setup and tap Unlock: one failed login and no more, `Rejected`, the record marked, no second try, and the sentence on the phone. What the Mac shows at the lock screen afterwards (a hint, a delay).
- **U9. The record and the notice.** What the history holds after each outcome, that it holds nothing of the secrets, and what the notification shows at the Mac. Whether the system lets this build, which is signed ad hoc, show a notification at all, what it asks the person when the unlock is first set up, and what the window says when the person answered no.
- **U10. The end.** End the session, drop the connection, quit the app and kill it, each before the face is asked for, while it is asked for, while the password is typed and after the unlock. Nothing is typed after the end, nothing is typed twice, and the Mac is locked in every case after the unlock.
- **U11. A Mac that is already locked.** Lock the Mac by hand, allow the phone to start a session on it, and ask for the screen from the phone: does the session start, does the Mac pause it as locked, do the cover and the hold come up, does the offer say `unlock`, and does the unlock then go as in U1 to U4. Where the session does not start, say how it fails and what the person sees.
- **U12. The box after an unlock.** After an unlock the Mac holds a new box and a new sealed key, and the key of the unlock opens nothing (a test build answers a new question with the old key and gets `Stale`, which ends the setup, so this is done last). Kill the app between the new pair and the first key typed, and tap Unlock again: the face is asked for again and the unlock works. Make the record unwritable and tap Unlock: nothing is typed, `Failed`, and the next tap works once it is writable again.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the password on the phone and send it at the unlock.** The password would travel, and the phone would hold it. A phone that is taken apart or a bug in a log would give it away.
- **Seal the password itself to the phone, with no box on the Mac.** It is one key fewer, and the password is then in the memory of the phone's app and on the wire at every unlock. With the box, what the phone handles is a key that opens nothing without the Mac's box.
- **A signed "the face was recognised" from the phone, with the password in the Keychain of the Mac.** The check of the face would be a promise that a changed or a compromised app can make without the face. Opening a key that only the Secure Enclave can open after the face makes the face a condition of the cryptography.
- **The passcode of the phone as a way round.** Someone who watched the passcode, and holds the phone, would open the Mac. The key is made without that way and a new face ends it.
- **The unlock that the system offers with a watch, or the Touch ID of a keyboard.** They are open to the system only.
- **A call or a module of the system that unlocks without typing.** None is documented. A plug-in to the authorisation or to the login window needs an administrator and a place in the system folders, and cannot be part of an app that is sandboxed.
- **A Mac that never locks.** The person asked that the Mac lock, and a Mac that cannot is the failure that ADR 0011 was written against.
- **Unlock in a session that is not private.** It would light the Mac and show the lock screen and the desktop to whoever is near it, which is what the person said must not happen.
- **Retry for the person.** A wrong password counts toward the limits of the system on failed logins, and a stale password fails every time. The person is asked to set it up again.
- **Hold the box key or the password in the app between unlocks.** A copy in memory is what an attacker who can read the app's memory looks for. They are made, used and wiped.
- **Send the password as keys through the input of the viewer.** The viewer would need the password, and the gate drops input at a locked Mac on purpose.
- **One key on the phone for every Mac.** A key for each Mac keeps one Mac's setup from opening another's, and unpairing a Mac takes its key with it. It holds only if the phone opens a sealed key with the key it made for the Mac of the session and tries no other (protocol section 9).
- **The same box key at every unlock.** It was the first shape of this record. The key is a bearer key, so one copy of it, made by an app on the phone that was changed after the setup or left in the buffers of the networking library, would open the box for ever, with the pairing and without the face. The Mac puts the password in a new box under a new key at every unlock instead, and keeps the public key of the phone to seal it to.

## Consequences

- **The rule "Baton never unlocks anything" is replaced.** Baton unlocks a Mac, for a paired phone that the person set up at the Mac, after the face of the person on that phone, in a private session, once for a tap. Nothing else unlocks, and no other platform is built to be unlocked. The rule had come from other tools that unlock without asking, and from the Linux call that any process of the person can make; neither applies to this.
- **A phone whose person's face is used by someone else can unlock the Mac.** The system asks for attention to the phone by default, which makes this hard while the person sleeps or looks away; that is a setting of the person, and Baton cannot read it. The person at the Mac is told of each unlock when the screens are next lit. This includes a Mac that locked before the session began, which is the case that the person meets most. A rule that no `unlock` is offered while the Mac is locked would keep that case out of reach of someone who holds the phone and the person's face: a session that starts on an unlocked Mac already controls it (the unlock needs a private session, and a private session needs control), so locking it and unlocking it again would gain nothing. The rule would leave open only a session that is running when the Mac locks, and it would take the button away where the person wants it most. This record offers the unlock either way.
- **The Mac takes the word of the phone that the key is in the Secure Enclave.** Nothing attests it. The face therefore guards against an app on the phone that was changed after the setup, and not against one that was changed before it. It guards less than it seems even then: the Mac replaces its box when a key has opened it and not before, so a key that the phone opened and did not send stays good for one unlock, at a time the app picks and without a face. A changed app that gets the face of the person once, for an attempt that it then cancels, can keep that key and send it in an attempt of its own.
- **The password is in the memory of the app at the Mac for the time it takes to type it**, a second or two (inferred), in a buffer that is wiped afterwards. A program that can read the memory of the app, a debugger that the person allowed or an administrator, could read it then. The Mac builds are signed ad hoc, so they do not have the protections of a notarised app.
- **At the setup the password also passes through the Flutter side of the app at the Mac.** The person types it into a text field, so it is a Dart string, held by the field (and by the history that the field keeps for undo) until the window is closed, and then it is in the buffer of the bridge that hands it to the library. Baton can wipe neither: they last until the memory is reused. It happens once for each setup, at the Mac, and not at an unlock. The field hides what is typed and turns off suggestions, autocorrect and the learning of the keyboard, and it is emptied as soon as the call is made, whatever came of it; the library wipes its own copy when the call ends. A program that can read the memory of the app could read the password in that time, as in the bullet above.
- **The box key is in the memory of the phone's app and of the Mac for a moment, and in the buffers of the networking library for as long as they are kept**, which Baton cannot wipe. The key opens nothing without the box, and the box it opens is replaced when the key is used, so a copy of the key opens nothing that the Mac holds afterwards. There is one exception: a Mac that cannot keep its new box types nothing and keeps the old one, which the key that was sent still opens until a later unlock replaces it. A copy of that key, made by a changed app on the phone or left in the buffers of the networking library, is then good for more than one thing. An app on the phone that kept it sends it in the next attempt, with the new challenge, since the Mac sends the same sealed key again and opens its own box with what comes back: that is one unlock without a face. With a copy of the box from the disk of the Mac as well, it is the password itself. The person at the Mac is told that the attempt failed. Someone who holds an earlier box as well, from a backup of the disk of the Mac, has the password as it was then, until the person changes it.
- **A new face, a reset, a restored backup or a deleted app ends the key on the phone.** The setup is then unusable, the phone says so, and only the person at the Mac can set it up again. That is by design: nothing on the phone can restore a key to the Mac.
- **A changed password ends the setup with one failed login.** The Mac cannot know that the password changed until it has been used.
- **A Mac that is asleep, restarted, or whose lid is shut cannot be unlocked.** The setting "Stay reachable while charging" is a later change that keeps a Mac on a charger from sleeping, and it is not part of this record. A session may start on a Mac that is already locked, and its offer says `unlock` like any private session's. Whether it can start there at all, with its capture, its cover and its guard, has not been tried: [ADR 0010](0010-sessions-that-the-viewer-starts.md) says that it gives no picture, and [locked-devices.md](../locked-devices.md) marks the capture of a locked Mac as not known. U11 starts one. Where it cannot start, nothing here makes it, and the unlock is for a session that is running when the Mac locks.
- **A lock screen that is not a plain password field is not handled**: a list of users, a field for a name, a smart card, a single sign-on that is not the password. The password goes into whatever has the keyboard, and the check that the Mac unlocked finds that it did not (`Rejected`) or that it could not type (`Failed`). U1 and U7 say which of these exist on the person's Mac.
- **The consent for Accessibility is needed**, as it is for control. A Mac without it does not offer the setting.
- **The tap on the phone, the face, the wake and the typing take seconds**, and the picture resumes when the Mac says it is unlocked. U1 measures it.
- **A display that wakes may light for up to a quarter second.** ADR 0011 gives the number for a display that wakes while the cover is up, and U3 checks it for this path.
- **The history can be deleted by anyone who can write the files of the person.** It is a record for the person, and not evidence against someone who has the Mac's disk.
- **A look-only session, and a session that the person started at the Mac, have no unlock.** Privacy follows control (ADR 0011), and the unlock follows privacy.
- **Locking and the screens are still ADR 0011's.** The guard locks the Mac if the app dies, an app that dies during an unlock is locked as at any other time, and nothing here changes what the guard does.
